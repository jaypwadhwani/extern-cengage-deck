The position

A spring pilot needs no integration work on either side. The deeper integration is worth scoping together once a student cohort has actually run.

Everything in Phase 1 uses a model Extern runs with university partners today. Phase 2 is the map of what a fuller integration involves, with the decisions that belong to Cengage called out. We have not put timelines against Phase 2, because several of those decisions are still open on both sides.

Where we stand today

Direct answers to the questions from the call

Taking these first so the phasing that follows is read against an accurate picture of the platform.

Do you have LTI?

No. LTI is the standard that lets an outside learning tool plug into a course platform as a native assignment. It is on our roadmap and it is not built today.

What about single sign-on?

Google sign-in works today. Additional standards-based options, including the federated identity model used across higher ed, are on our active roadmap. Single sign-on means a student uses credentials they already have rather than creating an Extern password.

Any automated courseware integrations today?

None. Every institution partner runs on the roster model on the next slide, which is deliberate. It is what lets a pilot start without an engineering project on either side.

What is the stack?

Node.js on AWS, React front end, JSON APIs described with OpenAPI. As Vijay noted on the call, that lines up closely with Cengage, which removes most of the usual integration friction.

Phase 1  ·  Spring 2027

How students get in

Four steps. No engineering on either side, because this is the same flow Extern runs with its university partners today.

Step 1

Instructor selects

The instructor chooses which students take an externship, and which programs fit the course.

Step 2

Roster to Extern

Cengage or the instructor sends the roster — the list of chosen students, as names and email addresses.

Step 3

We provision accounts

We create those accounts in advance and mark them as paid, so the student never meets a paywall.

Step 4

Student starts

The student registers with the email on the roster and goes straight into the program.

The roster is the gate, not the link. A link placed in a course is only a doorway. It carries no access of its own, so it cannot be forwarded to someone who should not have a seat. Anyone arriving without being on the roster sees the standard consumer paywall. This also means instructors do not have to manage access themselves.

Phase 1  ·  Spring 2027

What Cengage receives

Four things flow back to Cengage and to instructors during the pilot. All are available in spring without anything new being built.

ItemWhat it isHow it arrives in spring
Program catalog The list of externship programs and their attributes: company, subject area, skills, duration, weekly time commitment. A document today. A read-only data feed is available if you would rather ingest it electronically.
Program calendar Start and end dates for programs that are scheduled. Programs run on fixed dates and recur. Shared document, refreshed as terms are scheduled.
Instructor report Engagement, progress, students at risk of falling behind, and completion, for that instructor's section. Every two weeks, by email, with a summary view per section.
Competency mapping Which NACE competencies each program develops, and the ones we would recommend to instructors first. A matrix document, program by program.
Catalog — simply the list of programs we offer and the details of each one. Today a formatted document, and readable by machine if that is more useful to you.
NACE competencies — the standard career-readiness skill framework. Allie identified this as the core metadata Cengage's recommendation engine works from, which makes it the natural handshake between the two systems.

Two notes for the architecture conversation. start_date and end_date answer the question about whether externships are fixed or ongoing: they are fixed, and they recur, so a recommendation can be timed against a course's own dates. updated_at means you can pull only what has changed rather than refetching the whole catalog. Competency tags are the natural next field here, and that mapping is what Phase 2 turns into a live exchange rather than a document.

Phase 2  ·  After the pilot

Four workstreams, and what each one needs from Cengage

This is the map, not a schedule. Each of these depends on a decision that has not been made yet, which is why we have deliberately left timelines off.

1   Identity

Students arrive already signed in, with no separate Extern password to create or remember. Built on the federated identity standards higher ed already uses rather than consumer sign-in.

What we would need from youWhich identity provider, and whether students authenticate as Cengage users or as users of their own institution.

2   Catalog API

Instead of reading a list, your recommendation engine asks ours a question and gets back exactly the programs that fit — filtered by department and by when the course runs.

What we would need from youWhich filters your engine actually queries on, and the volume of calls to expect.

3   Competency exchange

Competency data moving both directions: which competencies a program develops on the way in, and what a student demonstrated on the way out. This is the loop Allie described.

What we would need from youYour competency schema, and how you represent a student's readiness today.

4   Surface

Whether Extern appears embedded inside MindTap, or launches as a linked tool through LTI. This is a product and UX decision before it is an engineering one.

What we would need from youThe outcome of your UX research. The authentication approach follows from this, as Vijay noted.

Why this order

Pilot first, then integrate

The usual publisher playbook is to complete the integration before anything launches. We would rather not, and the reasons are practical.

  1. 01

    Integration built ahead of demand gets built against assumptions

    Neither side knows yet which programs instructors actually recommend, how students move through them, or which parts of the workflow create friction. A spring cohort answers all three. Scoping afterwards means scoping against real usage rather than a best guess.

  2. 02

    The surface decision drives the identity decision

    This was Vijay's own point on the call. Whether Extern is embedded in MindTap or launched as a linked tool changes how authentication has to work. Until the UX research settles that, building the authentication layer means guessing at the answer.

  3. 03

    Nothing in Phase 1 has to be unwound

    The roster model produces the same enrolments, the same progress data and the same competency mapping that a deeper integration would automate. Phase 1 is not a throwaway path. It is the same journey with people in the loop where systems will later be.

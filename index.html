Direct answers to the questions from the call

Taking these first, so the phasing that follows is read against an accurate picture of the platform.

The standard that lets an outside learning tool plug into a course platform as a native assignment. On our roadmap, not built today.

Partly

Google sign-in works today. The federated identity model used across higher ed is on our active roadmap. Single sign-on means a student uses credentials they already have.

Phase 1 · Spring 2027

How students get in

Four steps, and no engineering on either side, because this is the same flow Extern runs with its university partners today.

1

Instructor selects

The instructor chooses which students take an externship, and which programs fit the course.

2

Roster to Extern

Cengage or the instructor sends the roster, the list of chosen students as names and email addresses.

3

We provision

We create those accounts in advance and mark them paid, so the student never meets a paywall.

4

Student starts

The student registers with the email on the roster and goes straight into the program.

The roster is the gate, not the link. A link placed in a course is only a doorway. It carries no access of its own, so it cannot be forwarded to someone without a seat, and anyone arriving without being on the roster sees the standard consumer paywall. It also means instructors never have to manage access themselves.

Three notes for the architecture conversation

  • start_date end_date Answers the question about fixed versus ongoing. Programs run on fixed dates and they recur, so a recommendation can be timed against a course's own calendar.
  • updated_at Lets you pull only what has changed rather than refetching the whole catalog on every sync.
  • competencies The natural next field here. That mapping arrives as a matrix in Phase 1, and becomes a live exchange in Phase 2 rather than a document.

Why this order

Pilot first, then integrate

The usual publisher playbook is to complete the integration before anything launches. We would rather not, and the reasons are practical.

  1. 01

    Integration built ahead of demand gets built against assumptions

    Neither side knows yet which programs instructors actually recommend, how students move through them, or where the workflow creates friction. A spring cohort answers all three, so scoping afterwards means scoping against real usage rather than a best guess.

  2. 02

    The surface decision drives the identity decision

    This was Vijay's own point on the call. Whether Extern is embedded in MindTap or launched as a linked tool changes how authentication has to work. Until the UX research settles that question, building the authentication layer means guessing at the answer.

  3. 03

    Nothing in Phase 1 has to be unwound

    The roster model produces the same enrolments, the same progress data and the same competency mapping a deeper integration would automate. Phase 1 is not a throwaway path. It is the same journey, with people in the loop where systems will later be.
